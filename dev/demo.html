Keeping a starter alive through a busy week

A notebook entry · 9 min read · 24 comments

A starter doesn’t need daily attention as much as it needs a routine that fits the days you actually have. The schedule below keeps one lively on two feedings a week, and leaves room for the evenings when the jar gets forgotten. If you’re new to this, start with your first starter and come back once it doubles reliably.

Feed less, feed colder

The trick is slowing the starter down rather than keeping up with it. A stiffer mix, about 60% hydration, ferments more slowly, and a spell in the fridge stretches the time between feedings to three or four days. Take it out the evening before you bake, give it a warm refresh, and it’s ready by morning.

When the week gets away from you

Grey liquid on top means it’s hungry, not ruined. Pour it off, keep a spoonful from the bottom, and feed it twice at room temperature. Most starters bounce back within a day; the troubleshooting notes cover the stubborn ones, and the starter log shows how ours recovered after ten days away.

A weekly rhythm

Sunday: feed and bake. Wednesday: feed and back in the fridge. That’s it. For loaves that fit around work, try the overnight loaf or the weekday focaccia, both shaped in under twenty minutes.
